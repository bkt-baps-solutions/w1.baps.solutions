The range is the point

One key for clinic notes, investor updates,
mandir work and everything else you write.

Most dictation tools ask you to pick a lane: a medical scribe, a meeting notetaker, a writing assistant. A working day does not pick a lane. w1 assumes one person carries several vocabularies and several tones, and that switching between them should cost one keypress.

Clinical

Notes, letters, handovers. Drug names, anatomy and abbreviations that a general model turns into nonsense, and a verbatim mode that never quietly improves what you said.

Startup

Founder updates, memos and product thinking spoken out loud on a walk, arriving as structure instead of mush.

Community

Gujarati terminology, names and sacred vocabulary with canonical spellings that mainstream tools get wrong every time.

Personal

Messages, journal entries and half-formed ideas at speed, where sounding like yourself is the entire requirement.

Modes

Same key, different destination.

Plain dictation gives you the transcript. The modes below take that transcript somewhere: into a prompt, into your own prose, into something you can send. Pick one from the menu bar or bind it to its own key.

Prompt Engineer

Think out loud and get a prompt back with the shape a model actually needs: the task, the context you mentioned in passing, the constraints you assumed, the output format you wanted. The mode most likely to save you a rewrite.

Saidright so i need something that looks at the last six months of referrals and tells me which clinics are running over, and i want it as a table i can paste into the board pack
TypedTask: analyse referral volume by clinic over the last six months and identify clinics exceeding capacity. Context: output goes into a board pack. Format: a table, one row per clinic, sorted by overrun. Flag any month with incomplete data rather than estimating it.

Polish

Clearer, not different. Repetition and false starts go, the sentence order gets fixed, and the words you chose stay the words you chose. Use it for anything a person will read once and reply to.

Saidso basically the thing is, um, we can't ship friday because the notarisation takes, like, a day and we haven't even, we haven't started it yet
TypedWe cannot ship on Friday. Notarisation takes about a day and we have not started it yet.

Formal

A light pass, not a rewrite. Punctuation, capitalisation, contractions opened up, dates and place names cased properly. Nothing is added, nothing is summarised. For letters, referrals and anything with a recipient who is not a friend.

Saidthanks for seeing mrs okafor on the fourteenth of july in london she'll need a repeat fbc in six weeks
TypedThank you for seeing Mrs Okafor on 14 July in London. She will need a repeat FBC in six weeks.

Street

Lowercase stays lowercase. Slang, shorthand and the words you actually use survive intact, because a message that reads like a press release reads like it was not written by you. Only the transcription errors get fixed.

Saidyeah lets push the seed deck to monday, i'll redo the metrics slide tonight, its the only bit thats actually weak
Typedyeah lets push the seed deck to monday, i'll redo the metrics slide tonight, it's the only bit that's actually weak

Examples are illustrative, written to show what each mode is for. Prompt Engineer, Polish, Formal and Street reshape the transcript, which is the layer that may use a language model beyond your machine. w1 tells you which layer a mode uses before it runs, and plain transcription always works on its own.

Vocabulary

A glossary layer, not a custom words box.

Most tools give you a handful of slots for names the model keeps mangling. w1 has a correction engine: give it a vocabulary and it enforces canonical spellings on the transcript, with rules, sources and tests behind them.

BAPS was the hardest test, not the target market

w1 started as a tool for one stubborn problem: getting Gujarati sacred terminology spelled correctly, every time, inside ordinary English text. Hundreds of terms, canonical spellings, a strict transliteration rule, and a domain where nearly right is simply wrong. Solving that properly meant building a real vocabulary layer instead of an autocorrect list. That layer is now what makes w1 get drug names, company names and your own jargon right too. The mandir glossary still ships. It is one vocabulary among several.

  • Canonicalisation, not substitution. A term has one correct spelling and w1 lands on it, whichever way you said it.
  • Sourced entries. Glossary entries carry their provenance, so a correction can be checked rather than trusted.
  • Your own list. Add the words your work depends on: a drug formulary, a cap table, a codebase, the names of people you email.
  • A verbatim mode that stays verbatim. When accuracy matters more than tidiness, no vocabulary pass runs at all.

Language and transliteration

English is the default. A menu switches to Gujarati or to Mixed, for the way people actually talk when two languages share a sentence.

jay svāminārāyan, āje kathā sāras hati
  • English output always carries transliterations, so a Gujarati term arrives in Roman script you can read, search and paste.
  • One diacritic, ever. The long a macron and nothing else. No dots, no underlines, no marks that break in someone else's font.

Honest status: Gujarati recognition accuracy is still being worked on. The language menu is there and the transliteration rule is settled, but w1 is not claiming Gujarati is solved today. Training data comes next.

Meetings

A transcript that ends up in your notes, not someone else's product.

w1 captures your microphone and the incoming system audio together, shows a live transcript with speaker labels while the meeting runs, and can join a Teams, Google Meet or Zoom call on its own.

Where the summary goes

When the meeting ends, the notes are routed to one of your contexts. w1 reads that context before it writes: it updates the notes you already have, frames the summary against what is already there, and generates tasks that fit the project rather than restating the call.

baps.solutions Community and mandir work

Meeting summarisation reads a transcript and writes prose, so it uses the model layer, not only the on-device one. It says so before it runs, and the raw transcript is yours either way.

Privacy

Two layers, stated plainly.

Plenty of tools claim nothing ever leaves your computer. That claim is only true if the tool does nothing but transcribe. Here is where the line actually sits in w1.

On your Mac, unconditionally

  • Whisper runs locally through Apple MLX. Audio is turned into text on the machine.
  • The glossary and correction pass are local code over local data.
  • Plain dictation works offline, with no account, after the model has been downloaded once.
  • Insights are computed from your own history on disk.

The layer that rewrites

  • Prompt Engineer, Polish, Formal, Street and meeting summarisation work on the transcript, and may use a language model that does not run on your Mac.
  • Every mode names the layer it uses before it runs. Nothing is sent quietly.
  • Turn the rewriting layer off entirely and w1 remains a complete dictation tool.
  • Anything involving patient information stays in verbatim dictation. That path never leaves the device.

w1 needs three macOS permissions and no more: Microphone to hear you, Accessibility to paste into the focused app, and Input Monitoring so the global hotkey works. macOS will ask for each one, and you can see exactly why in the source.

Install

Two ways in.

w1 is built for Apple Silicon. On an Intel Mac it falls back to a slower CPU transcription backend rather than MLX, and everything else works the same.

Testers: the app

Signed and notarized

A disk image, signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it opens without a Gatekeeper warning and keeps its permissions across updates.

1Download W1.dmg from the latest release and open it.
2Drag W1.app into Applications, then launch it from there.
3Grant the three permissions when macOS asks. w1 lives in the menu bar.
4First launch downloads the Whisper model once, about 1.5 GB, and caches it locally.
Microphone Accessibility Input Monitoring
Download W1.dmg

No Homebrew formula and no App Store listing. The disk image is the distribution.

Developers: from source

install.sh

The installer creates a Python 3.12 virtual environment, installs w1 with the MLX and macOS extras, pre-downloads the Whisper model and runs an environment check. It needs uv on your path. 

# clone and install
git clone https://github.com/osx-earth/w1.osx.earth.git w1
cd w1
./install.sh

# launch the menu bar app
./w1 app

# check the environment at any time
./w1 doctor

Building your own W1.app is one more step: packaging/build.sh --dmg. Signing and notarization details are in docs/PACKAGING.md.
